1

Make a plan

  1. Type the exam date in the first box. The next two sections appear once you do.
  2. Set the hours you can really study in a day, and tap any weekdays you won't study.
  3. Paste your topic list — one topic per line — into the box in section 2 and press Add these.
  4. Adjust the topics. Tap a dot for how shaky you feel (one dot is shaky, five is solid), and S / M / L for how big the topic is.
  5. Read the plan that appears in section 3. It is already built — there is nothing else to press.

In a hurry? The See a sample plan button on the first screen loads a filled-in example in one tap, so you can see what you get before typing anything.

2

Put it in your calendar

No account to connect. You download one file and open it.

  1. Set when you start studying — the Study starts at box in section 1. That is the time the first session lands on. Change it if mornings aren't your thing.
  2. Scroll past the plan to the row of buttons: Print · Rebalance · Start over.
  3. Tap Export & backup, the fold-out just under those buttons.
  4. Tap Calendar file .ics. A file called plan-it-2026-11-13.ics saves to your device.
  5. Open that file. What happens next depends on the app you have — see below.

On an iPhone or iPad

The file itself is fine — iOS just refuses to hand a downloaded .ics straight to Calendar. If your share sheet has no Calendar option, don't fight it: import through iCloud instead. It is four taps, on the same phone.

Route 1 — iCloud Calendar in Safari (most reliable on iPhone)

  1. In Safari, go to icloud.com/calendar and sign in.
  2. Tap the calendars button, or the gear at the bottom of the calendar list.
  3. Choose Import vCal… and pick the .ics from your Downloads.
  4. Choose which calendar it goes into, then Import. The sessions appear in the Calendar app on your phone within a minute.

Route 2 — the share sheet, if your phone offers it

  1. Open Files → Downloads and tap the .ics.
  2. Tap the share button, then look for Calendar. If it isn't in the row, tap More and switch Calendar on.
  3. That opens the import sheet — Add All sits at the top, or at the bottom of the list if the list is long. Scroll if you don't see it.

Route 3 — Mail

  1. Email the .ics to yourself from wherever it downloaded.
  2. Open the message in Mail and tap the attachment — Mail opens the import sheet directly.

If none of those work: import it once on a computer — Google Calendar, or Apple Calendar on a Mac (below) — and let it reach the phone by syncing. That path has never failed, and you only do it once per plan.

On Android

  1. Open your Downloads notification, or Files → Downloads.
  2. Tap the .ics file.
  3. If asked what to open it with, choose Calendar. Then tap Add on the sessions.

Google Calendar (do this on a computer)

  1. Open calendar.google.com in a browser.
  2. Click the gear (top right) → Settings.
  3. In the left menu, click Import & export.
  4. Under Import, click Select file from your computer and choose the .ics.
  5. Pick which calendar it goes into, then click Import. It appears on your phone within a minute or two.

The Google Calendar phone app can't import files — that is a limit of the app, not of this one. Import once on the web and everything syncs.

Apple Calendar on a Mac

  1. Double-click the .ics, or use File → Import inside Calendar.
  2. Choose which calendar it goes into, then click Add All.
  3. If you use iCloud, it reaches your phone on its own.

Outlook

  1. On the web: Calendar → Add calendar → Upload from file → choose the .ics.
  2. Desktop app: File → Open & Export → Import an iCalendar (.ics) → choose the file.

What is inside the file

One event per session, with the topic as the title and the subject as the category. A day that starts at 09:00 with a 45-minute session, a 10-minute break and a 30-minute session arrives as 09:00–09:45 and 09:55–10:25. The exam date is an all-day event, and anything sitting in If you get time is listed in that event's notes.

The times carry no timezone on purpose, so 09:00 means nine in the morning wherever you are.

3

Print it

  1. Tap Print in the row of buttons under the plan.
  2. Your browser's print window opens. To keep it as a file instead of paper, choose Save as PDF as the printer.
  3. Print. It comes out one week to a page, with the checkboxes showing so you can tick them with a pen.
4

Keep a copy

Your plan lives in this browser. A saved copy is the only thing that survives clearing it.

  1. Tap Export & backup → Save a copy .json.
  2. Keep the file somewhere that isn't this browser — email it to yourself, drop it in Drive.

To bring it back: open Plan It, tap Restore a copy, and pick the file. The topics, hours, days off and every tick come back exactly as they were. On a fresh visit the same link sits on the first screen, next to See a sample plan.

To move it to another device: save the copy on the first device, send it across, restore it on the second. A file that isn't a Plan It backup is refused with a plain message rather than loading half a plan.

5

What each part does

In section 1 — the deadline

Exam or submission date
The day you're working towards. Nothing is scheduled on it or after it.
Hours a day you can really study
The number you'd honestly hit on a normal day. Every day is capped at this.
Study starts at
Only used by the calendar file, so sessions get real clock times. It doesn't change the plan.
Days off
Tap the weekdays you won't study. Nothing lands there — not even a review.

In section 2 — the topics

Subject
Free text, remembered as you type. It groups the timetable and stops the same subject landing back to back.
The five dots
Confidence. One dot is shaky and earns the topic more time; five is nearly there and earns it less.
S / M / L
Rough first-pass work: about 30 minutes, an hour, two hours. Confidence then scales it.

In section 3 — the plan

Ticking a session
Saved the moment you tick it. The strip in the margin fills in as you go.
Rebalance
For after you miss a few days. Everything unticked — including days that have passed — is rescheduled into the days that are left. Ticked sessions stay where they are, and whatever no longer fits moves to If you get time. Nothing is deleted.
If you get time
At the bottom of the plan: the topics past what the calendar can hold. Not scheduled, not lost.
Where each topic stands
A fold-out list of every topic with its sessions and the time left on it.
Start over
Clears the topics and the plan from this browser. Save a copy first if you might want it back.

Your data

Everything is kept in this browser, in one saved plan. No account, no server, nothing uploaded. That also means clearing your browser's site data clears the plan, and a different browser or device starts empty — a saved .json copy is the only thing that crosses that gap.
